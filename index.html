Card payments inside Odoo Point of Sale

Trippy Tacos, US food truck and catering2026
In sandbox testing

  • Odoo 17
  • Python
  • OWL / JavaScript
  • Authorize.net
  • Docker
  • AWS EC2
The problem
Odoo's built-in Authorize.net integration only covers invoices and online orders, not in-person sales. Staff needed to take cards in Odoo POS without extra terminal hardware or third-party apps.
What I built
  • A custom module, authorize_net_pos, extending pos.payment.method with credential fields and a server-side charge method.
  • A POS payment handler extending Odoo 17's PaymentInterface. Accept.js tokenizes the card in the browser, so raw card numbers never reach the server.
  • An isolated Docker Compose clone of the Odoo 17 and PostgreSQL 16 stack on the production EC2 server, so development never touched the live POS.
  • Traced a silent bug where POS reported success without contacting Authorize.net, by reading Odoo core and the Adyen module, then fixed it with a targeted override.
Outcome
Payments now route through the custom handler in sandbox, with zero downtime for the restaurant's live POS throughout development.

Automated customer re-engagement, and an email bug nobody had spotted

Trippy Tacos, US food truck and catering2026
Live in production

  • Odoo 17 Community
  • Python
  • QWeb
  • ir.cron
  • PostgreSQL
The problem
Odoo's Marketing Automation is Enterprise-only, and the business runs Community. The team needed timely follow-ups with past catering clients. While building it, I found customers were receiving blank-looking emails.
What I built
  • A custom module that adds a tracked Last Event Date, five outreach categories and a reminder model with unlimited follow-up dates per customer.
  • A daily scheduled job that sends the right email template, marks each reminder sent and logs it on the contact, plus an Outreach tab on CRM opportunities that syncs both ways.
  • Database analysis showed 470 of 777 incoming emails were being relayed back to their own senders. I fixed it permanently by overriding the lead's notification recipients, and stopped a separate hourly mail loop.
Outcome
Verified end to end with an unattended production run across all five categories. The first live customer replies after the fix had zero echoes.

One CRM thread from quote to paid invoice

Trippy Tacos, US food truck and cateringModule: crm_create_invoice
In daily use

  • Odoo
  • Python
  • QWeb portal
  • Authorize.net
The problem
Quotes, invoices, signatures and payments were spread across CRM, Sales and Accounting, so staff juggled three apps for every booking.
What I built

Rescuing a 6,177-product store from a compromised domain

UK multi-brand e-commerce group2026
Completed

  • WooCommerce
  • WP-CLI
  • SQL
  • ImageMagick
  • rsync / SSH
The problem
A 6,177-product store with 1,063 variations sat on a malware-compromised domain, and earlier import attempts had left 2,978 duplicate products behind.
What I built
  • Re-encoded and sanitized 31,854 images with ImageMagick, stripped their metadata, and moved them over SSH with rsync through network drops.
  • Diagnosed four stacked import failures: stale image URLs, a Varnish cache blocking bulk fetches, a WP-CLI importer parameter bug and mangled file paths.
  • Generated deterministic SKUs for 1,063 variations that had none.
  • Verified the result with a raw SQL scan of all 182 text and blob columns.
Outcome
A clean catalog on the new domain with zero duplicates and zero references to the old domain anywhere in the database.

  • Traced broken thumbnails on 115 products to dead supplier URLs in a variant metafield, then repaired them.
Outcome
Close to 300 styles live in the store with front, back and side views, and the automation code moved into the client's own GitHub organization.

How I work

The same six steps on every project, whether it's a one-off fix or a system the business will run for years.

  1. Understand the business first

    Scope with the owner and the people who'll use it, and confirm who signs off before I write code.

  2. Design the system before building

    Map the workflow, decide what to customize and what the platform already does, and agree on a phased plan with clear milestones.

  3. Build away from production

    Staging clones, backups before every change and read-only checks, so the live business never stops.

  4. Verify before calling it done

    Test end to end on real flows. I only report what's confirmed working, and say plainly what's still pending.

  5. Launch and train the team

    Go live in a controlled release, walk the team through it, and turn their feedback into fixes in the first weeks.

  6. Support and improve long-term

    Daily end-of-day updates, SOPs and handover files, plus ongoing improvements as the business grows.
